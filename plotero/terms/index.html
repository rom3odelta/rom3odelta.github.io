1. What the App is, and what it is not

1.1 A drawing tool and a deal workspace. Plotero reads the technical description on a land title, the tie line from a monument and the bearing-and-distance legs that follow it, and draws the shape those numbers describe. It reports area, perimeter and closure error, and it can put that drawing over satellite imagery. You can group properties into portfolios, keep the files and photos that go with a deal, and share a portfolio with other people. What you save lives on your device unless you back it up or share it.

1.2 Plotero is a visualization, not a survey. This is the most important thing in these Terms. Every plot the App draws or exports carries the line "Visualization only, not a certified survey", and that line is exact. Plotero does not perform a survey. It does not locate, verify, restore or place a single monument on the ground. It does not produce a document any registry, court, bank or government office is obliged to accept, and nothing it outputs is a substitute for work done and signed by a licensed Geodetic Engineer.

1.3 What the drawing depends on. The shape is only ever as good as the numbers behind it. Those numbers come from a document you supply, read either by the App's scanner or typed in by you. Old titles carry typographical errors, transcription errors and descriptions that do not close. A scanner can misread a faint or skewed page. Plotero shows you the closure error so you can see when something is off, but it cannot tell you whether the title itself is correct, current, authentic, or describes the land you think it does.

1.4 Decisions are yours. Do not buy, sell, mortgage, subdivide, fence, build on, or bring a claim about land on the strength of a Plotero drawing. Have the property surveyed and have the title verified at the Registry of Deeds. You are responsible for how you use what the App shows you, and for anything you share with someone else.

1.5 What needs the network. Drawing, editing, browsing, measuring and exporting run on your device with no network. Reading a title, the map and satellite imagery, backup, sharing and your account all need a connection. The privacy policy describes what each one sends.
