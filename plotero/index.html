What this app is

Plotero draws the shape of a land lot from the bearings and distances written in a Philippine land title's technical description. You photograph the title and Plotero reads it, or you type the description in yourself. The app plots the boundary, closes it, reports the area, and can put the shape on a map. It is a drawing and checking tool for landowners and brokers. It is not a survey instrument, and it does not produce an official survey document.

In short

Plotero collects nothing about you. Everything you type and everything Plotero draws stays on your phone, unless you choose to back it up. A backup goes to storage you already own, not to us.

A few things do leave your phone, and each one only when you ask for it: reading a title, finding a printed line on the page, naming a document off its heading, and putting satellite imagery under a drawing. What Plotero sends explains exactly what goes out and what comes back.

There is an optional sign-in. You never have to use it. Every part of Plotero works signed out.

What Plotero stores

When you save a lot, Plotero writes it to your device's own app storage. A saved lot contains:

  • the bearings and distances you entered, which is the technical description of the boundary;
  • the tie line and the monument as your title names it, for example BLLM No. 1, Mp. of Montalban, Rizal;
  • an optional nickname you type yourself, and the date you saved the lot;
  • what the title says about itself, on the Title details sheet: the title number and type, the registered owner's name, the lot, block and plan numbers, the location, the area as printed, the survey dates and the adjoining lots;
  • and, only if you pressed Read the exact wording, the verbatim technical description.

From version 0.27, if you send somebody a map link, the words you chose to put on that link are saved with the lot so the next link says the same thing. They are your own words, so they are kept and backed up like a folder name is. Leave that box blank and nothing is stored.

From version 0.21 you can also sort saved lots into folders. The folder's name is whatever you typed. If you name one after a family, that name is stored on your phone and goes into your backup like anything else here. A folder holds no other information.

From version 0.29 you can put several lots into one drawing, called a plot. Its name works the same way: whatever you typed, stored on your phone, in your backup. The only other thing a plot holds is where you dragged each lot, which is a distance north and east in metres, measured against the other lots on the same sheet. That is not a location on Earth and does not turn into one. Where your land actually sits comes from the map pin described below, and from nowhere else.

Up to version 0.19 the second half of that list was not saved. Those details were shown on screen and thrown away when you closed the lot, which meant photographing the same title twice to get a name back. From version 0.20 they are kept with the lot, so a lot in your library is a record of the paper rather than only of its shape. They are kept on your phone, and they are in your backup.

They are still not printed on the PDF you export. A plan you send to somebody else carries the drawing, the measurements and the name you typed, and nothing that was printed on the title.

From version 0.29 a lot can be one of several on a drawing. If it is, the sheet also carries the other lots as outlines, each one's name, and a line per lot saying how it got where it is, plus the drawing's name if you gave it one. Those are all names you typed. Nothing off the title goes on it.

You can read and correct all of it on the Title details sheet, and deleting the lot deletes it.

Backing up

Plotero offers two ways to keep a copy of your saved lots. Both are optional, and neither sends anything to us.

To a file. Plotero writes your saved lots to a .json file and hands it to you. Where it goes after that is entirely your business. This needs no account and works offline.

To your own Google Drive. If you sign in with Google, Plotero keeps a copy of your saved lots in your Drive. It asks for one permission, drive.appdata, which gives an app a private folder of its own inside your Drive and access to nothing else. Plotero cannot see, list or touch any other file you keep there. That folder is hidden from the normal Drive view; you can inspect or remove it from your Google account's settings for connected apps.

What goes into that copy is exactly what is on your phone: everything listed under What Plotero stores, including the title details, the registered owner's name and your folder and plot names. From version 0.20 that is a real change: before it, a backup held only geometry and nicknames. If you would rather those details never left the phone, clear them on the Title details sheet, or do not sign in and use file backup instead. The photograph of a title is never part of a backup.

When you sign in, the app reads the email address and name on the account so it can show you which account it is backing up to. That stays on the device. Plotero stores no access token: it asks Android for a short-lived one at the moment it writes to Drive, and holds nothing afterwards.

Google stores the backup, so Google's own privacy policy covers it while it sits there. We never see it.

Restoring is always something you ask for. Plotero will not pull a backup down and overwrite what is on your phone on its own, and a restore only ever adds lots or updates ones you already have. It never deletes.

Sign-in is available in the Android app. The web version has file backup only.

Where you are

Three things in Plotero use your phone's location, and they do different things with it. Your phone asks for permission the first time you tap into one of them, and not before. Everything else (reading a title, drawing the lot, browsing the map, searching, placing it on the map by hand, exporting) works with location refused, and will keep working that way.

Walkaround shows where you are standing against a lot you have already placed. Your position draws the blue dot and works out how far you are from the boundary, and that is all it does. It is not saved with the lot, it is not put in your backup, and it is not sent anywhere. Close the screen and Plotero stops asking your phone where you are.

Placing a lot from a corner is the other one, and here your position is the thing you are asking to keep. You stand on a corner of the land, tell Plotero which corner it is, and that reading becomes where the lot sits on the map. So it is saved with the lot, exactly like a placement you make by hand, and it is in your backup if you make one. It still goes nowhere else: we have no copy of it.

Find properties near me, the target button on the Map tab, is the third. It reads your position once, marks which of your own properties are close to it, and moves the map. The reading is not saved with anything and is not in your backup; the next time you press the button it asks your phone again.

You can take the permission back at any time in Android's app settings. Only those three things stop working, and a lot you already placed stays where you put it.

A property entrance is a point you choose by hand, not a reading from your phone. You drag the map until a pin sits on the gate or the road access, and Plotero keeps it so directions lead there instead of to the middle of the field. It is saved with the lot and it is in your backup if you make one, the same as a placement. It goes nowhere else, and you can remove it.

Sending a map link puts the lot's location in a message you write to somebody else. Nothing about that reaches us; it is your phone's own share sheet handing the text to whichever app you pick, and where it goes after that is between you and them. Before it is sent you are shown the exact message and can change every word of it, which is there so a link can go out without the name you file the lot under. The link points at the middle of the lot and carries no title number, no owner name and nothing else off the paper.

Making a satellite plan sends where the land is, because there is no other way to fetch a picture of it. It goes to Plotero's reader and on to the company that publishes the imagery, as a square of map and nothing else: no name, no title number, nothing saying whose land it is or who asked for it. See What Plotero sends.

What Plotero sends

Your saved lots are never sent to us, with or without an account. Nothing Plotero has drawn or stored on your phone leaves it on its own.

Reading a title is the exception, and it happens only when you press Smart Scan. That sends every page of the document you have open, one page at a time, to Plotero's reader, which passes each one to Google's document-reading service to pick out what is printed on it.

What comes back is what the title says about itself: the title number, the registered owner's name, the lot and block number, the plan number, the location, the area, the survey dates, the adjoining lots, the tie line and its monument, and the list of bearings and distances. The verbatim technical description, the long block of prose, is not part of an ordinary scan. Plotero's reader is told not to transcribe it, and refuses to pass it back even if it arrives.

There is one button that changes that. Read the exact wording, on the Title details sheet, sends the pages a second time and asks for that paragraph word for word. It is off unless you press it, it is the only way that text reaches the app, and what comes back is saved with the lot like the rest of the details.

Plotero's reader keeps none of it either way: it reads the page, hands the answer back, and holds nothing. Nothing from a scan is stored on our side.

The page you send is not stored, not by us and not as a copy anywhere on our side. We count how many reads happened and how long they took, so we can tell when the reader is broken. That count holds nothing about you or about the document, not even a fingerprint of it. Google's handling of the page is covered by Google's own terms; what they receive is the image, and nothing that says who you are.

Tapping a line to find it on the paper sends that page as well. On a lot read from a title, tapping a side highlights the row that describes it, and the only way to know where that row is printed is to let the reader look. Same reader, same rule: it answers and holds nothing. It happens once per page, and the answer is kept on your phone, so tapping the same row again sends nothing.

Naming a document by dragging a box over its heading sends the piece you boxed and only that piece, to read the words inside it. The heading comes back as the file's name. Nothing else on the page goes with it.

A satellite plan sends the patch of ground it is a picture of. The second sheet, the one with the land under the drawing, needs squares of imagery that cover your lot. Plotero asks its own reader for them and the reader fetches them from Esri, who publish the pictures. Asking for a square of the map says where that square is, and for this sheet that is where your land is. It carries nothing else with it, nothing is kept on our side, and Esri are handed a map coordinate the same way any map app hands them one.

If you never press any of those, nothing is ever sent, and the rest of the app works with the phone in airplane mode.

The only other thing Plotero may send is a request for a small file asking whether a newer version of the app exists, and only in builds where that check is switched on. That request carries no information about you or your lots. Like any request to a website it reveals your device's IP address and which version you are running, and nothing is stored about it.

Third parties

Plotero contains no advertising, no analytics, and no third-party tracking libraries.

Google is involved in two ways, and neither one happens unless you start it. If you sign in, your backup is kept in your own Google Drive. If you press Smart Scan, or tap a line to find it, or box a heading to name a file, the page goes to Google's document-reading service.

Esri is the third, and the same rule holds. Make a satellite plan and the map squares it needs are fetched from them, through us. Nothing else about you reaches anyone else.

Your control

Saved lots are removed when you delete them in the app, or when you uninstall Plotero.

If you used Drive, the app has a button that deletes the backup from your Google Drive. You can also sign out, which stops any further backing up, or revoke Plotero's access entirely at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Revoking removes our access but leaves the folder behind, so use the delete button first if you want it gone.

Because we never receive any of it, there is nothing for us to delete on our side and no copy for us to hand over to anyone.

Children

Plotero is a property tool intended for adults. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect any data from children.
