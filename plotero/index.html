What this app is

Plotero draws the shape of a land lot from the bearings and distances written in a Philippine land title's technical description. You type or paste that description, and the app plots the boundary, closes it, and reports the area. It is a drawing and checking tool for landowners and brokers. It is not a survey instrument, and it does not produce an official survey document.

In short

Plotero has no server and collects nothing about you. Everything you type and everything Plotero draws stays on your phone, unless you choose to back it up, and a backup goes to storage you already own rather than to us.

There is an optional sign-in. You never have to use it. Every part of Plotero works signed out.

What Plotero stores on your device

When you save a lot, Plotero writes it to your device's own app storage. A saved lot contains only:

  • the bearings and distances you entered, which is the technical description of the boundary;
  • the tie line and the designation of the monument it starts from, for example BLLM No. 1;
  • an optional nickname you type yourself, and the date you saved the lot.

That is the whole record. Plotero deliberately does not keep the registered owner's name, the title number, the plan number, or the municipality. Those are stripped out when a title is read, and the original text of a title is never saved. What remains is a shape and its measurements, which on its own does not identify a person or a parcel.

Backing up

Plotero offers two ways to keep a copy of your saved lots. Both are optional, and neither sends anything to us.

To a file. Plotero writes your saved lots to a .json file and hands it to you. Where it goes after that is entirely your business. This needs no account and works offline.

To your own Google Drive. If you sign in with Google, Plotero keeps a copy of your saved lots in your Drive. It asks for one permission, drive.appdata, which gives an app a private folder of its own inside your Drive and access to nothing else. Plotero cannot see, list or touch any other file you keep there. That folder is hidden from the normal Drive view; you can inspect or remove it from your Google account's settings for connected apps.

What goes into that copy is exactly what is on your phone: the same bearings, distances, nicknames and dates listed above, with the same details left out. The photograph of a title is never part of it, and neither is the text read from one.

When you sign in, the app reads the email address and name on the account so it can show you which account it is backing up to. That stays on the device. Plotero stores no access token: it asks Android for a short-lived one at the moment it writes to Drive, and holds nothing afterwards.

Google stores the backup, so Google's own privacy policy covers it while it sits there. We never see it.

Restoring is always something you ask for. Plotero will not pull a backup down and overwrite what is on your phone on its own, and a restore only ever adds lots or updates ones you already have. It never deletes.

Sign-in is available in the Android app. The web version has file backup only.

What Plotero sends

There is no Plotero server, so your lots are never sent to us, with or without an account. Aside from a backup you ask for, the app works fully with the phone in airplane mode.

The only other thing Plotero may send is a request for a small file asking whether a newer version of the app exists, and only in builds where that check is switched on. That request carries no information about you or your lots. Like any request to a website it reveals your device's IP address and which version you are running, and nothing is stored about it.

Third parties

Plotero contains no advertising, no analytics, and no third-party tracking libraries. Google is involved only if you sign in, and only as the place your own backup is kept.

Your control

Saved lots are removed when you delete them in the app, or when you uninstall Plotero.

If you used Drive, the app has a button that deletes the backup from your Google Drive. You can also sign out, which stops any further backing up, or revoke Plotero's access entirely at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Revoking removes our access but leaves the folder behind, so use the delete button first if you want it gone.

Because we never receive any of it, there is nothing for us to delete on our side and no copy for us to hand over to anyone.

Children

Plotero is a property tool intended for adults. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect any data from children.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Plotero ever sends anything to us, for example if reading a title photograph comes to rely on an online service we run, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the app will tell you what is being sent. The effective date at the top refers to the current version of this policy.
